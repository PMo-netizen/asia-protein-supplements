Markets

Hong Kong first. Then the region. China as a channel.

We list where we can stand behind the pack. We do not ask a partner to carry a market we have not prepared. This is a commercial sequence — not a regulatory playbook, and not a timeline in months.

The sequence

Hong Kong is the first market. Singapore, Japan, and Korea follow as the range is ready. Mainland China opens through cross-border e-commerce as a beachhead — a channel, not a claim that domestic registration is already in hand.

Why each market matters

HK

First market

Hong Kong

The listing we will prove. Sold as food. Traditional Chinese and English on pack. A bilingual grocery and drugstore trade that already runs protein — and the shelf a regional partner can point to later.

For a retailer: a food-positioned line written for this city's labelling, not a translated import. For a distributor: a house that is already here, not a Western brand adding Asia as a later chapter.

SG

Next

Singapore

The same line, prepared for Singapore retail once Hong Kong is set. English-led labelling, a concentrated grocery trade, and buyers who already compare regional items.

For a retailer: a second listing that does not fight the first on pack or claim. For a distributor: a territory that can follow Hong Kong without waiting for a separate brand story.

JP

Later

Japan

Pack and claims written for the Japanese trade, not translated at the last mile. The protein set is already dense; we will not arrive with a generic English master and a hope.

For a retailer: copy that can sit in a Japanese set. For a distributor: a counterpart who will write for the market, rather than hand over a finished imported SKU.

KR

Later

Korea

Same order of work: the range, then the market, then the conversation with local partners. Korea is not a copy of Japan, and we will not treat it as one.

For a retailer: a line that arrives when the pack is ready for that trade. For a distributor: a named conversation, not a regional blanket.

CN

Cross-border first

China

Cross-border e-commerce as a beachhead — Tmall Global, JD Worldwide, and related platforms — while general trade remains a later discussion. A channel choice, not a domestic-registration claim.

For a platform or CBEC distributor: a line that can sit in the cross-border aisle. For anyone asking about general trade: we will say on the call that it is later, and we will not pretend otherwise.

What this page is not

It is not a month-count, a consultant fee, or a lecture on registration routes. Those conversations, if they belong at all, belong after there is a commercial fit.
